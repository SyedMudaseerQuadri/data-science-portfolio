Who leaves a telecom, and can we catch them in time?

A telecom loses about one customer in four. I explored 7,043 customer records to find what separates those who leave from those who stay. Then I trained a Random Forest to flag at-risk customers, and retuned it until it caught 70% of real churners instead of 48%.

壱

The question

Winning a new subscriber costs a telecom far more than keeping an existing one. So the useful question isn't "how many people left?" It's "which customers are about to leave, and why?" That breaks down into two jobs:

  • Explain: find the customer traits linked to churn, so the business knows where to act.
  • Predict: score each customer's churn risk so a retention team can reach them before they cancel.
弐

The data

The IBM Telco Customer Churn dataset has one row per customer: 7,043 customers and 21 columns. The columns cover four areas:

GroupColumns
Accounttenure (months), Contract, PaperlessBilling, PaymentMethod, MonthlyCharges, TotalCharges
ServicesPhoneService, MultipleLines, InternetService, OnlineSecurity, OnlineBackup, DeviceProtection, TechSupport, StreamingTV, StreamingMovies
Demographicsgender, SeniorCitizen, Partner, Dependents
TargetChurn: Yes (1,869) / No (5,174)

The classes are imbalanced: only 26.5% of rows are churners. That matters later, because a model can score 73% accuracy by always predicting "stays" and never catch anyone.

参

Cleaning

df.info() showed that TotalCharges was stored as text, not numbers. Converting it with pd.to_numeric(errors="coerce") exposed 11 blank values. All 11 were brand-new customers with tenure = 0, so they had never been billed. I dropped them, leaving 7,032 rows, which is 0.16% of the data.

For modelling, I mapped Churn to 1/0, dropped the customerID column and one-hot encoded the categorical columns with pd.get_dummies(drop_first=True). That produced 30 numeric features.

四

Exploration

I started with the commitment a customer has made: their contract.

Insight

88.6% of all churners were on month-to-month plans. Any retention plan that ignores contract length is aiming at the wrong customers.

Next I looked at the add-on services. Customers without tech support or online security churned at almost three times the rate of customers who had them.

Then I looked at time. Churn is concentrated in the first few months of a customer's life.

Finally, I compared monthly charges between the two groups.

Insight

The highest-risk profile is a new, month-to-month, fiber customer paying by electronic check (45.3% churn) with no support add-ons. Each of those traits is something the business can change through pricing or onboarding.

五

Baseline model

I split the data 80/20 (random_state=42) and trained a RandomForestClassifier with 100 trees. Random Forest handles mixed numeric and one-hot features without scaling, and it shows which features it relies on.

On paper, 78.5% accuracy and a ROC-AUC of 0.816 look fine. The confusion matrix tells a different story: the model missed 196 of the 374 churners (52%). For a retention team, a missed churner is the expensive mistake. It's a customer who leaves without anyone reaching out.

One caution on Fig 6: impurity-based importance favours continuous columns like charges and tenure over 0/1 dummies. Contract type looks weaker here than it did in the EDA partly for that reason.

六

Catching more churners

When I re-ran the project for this case study, I tested one change to fix the recall problem. I set class_weight="balanced" so each churner counts about three times as much during training, and min_samples_leaf=5 so the trees can't memorise single customers. Everything else stayed the same: same split, same features.

The trade-off

The balanced model catches 84 more real churners in exchange for 128 more false alarms, and accuracy drops 3 points. A false alarm costs a retention email or a small discount. A missed churner costs all of their future revenue. For this use, the balanced model is the better choice, even though its accuracy is lower.

七

Recommendations

01 · CONTRACTS

Move month-to-month customers onto annual plans

Offer a discount or a perk for switching to a one-year contract. Churn falls from 42.7% to 11.3% when customers make that commitment.

02 · ONBOARDING

Focus on the first 90 days

32% of churners leave within three months. A welcome check-in and early-life offers should target this window.

03 · ADD-ONS

Bundle tech support and security

Customers with either add-on churn at a third of the rate. Free trials for new fiber customers could test whether this effect is causal.

04 · OPERATIONS

Score customers monthly

Run the balanced model monthly and pass the top-risk list to the retention team. Use the model's probability to rank who to contact first.

八

Limits & next steps

  • Correlation, not cause. Customers who buy support add-ons may already be more committed. An A/B test of free trials would show whether the add-ons themselves reduce churn.
  • One snapshot. The data has no timestamps, so the model can't see changes in behaviour before someone leaves, such as a recent price increase or support calls.
  • Next: tune the decision threshold against a real cost per false alarm, compare against logistic regression and gradient boosting, and use permutation importance for a fairer feature ranking.
