What Netflix offers, and what it costs to stay.

Retention on a streaming service comes down to two things: is there something worth watching, and is it worth the price? I studied both. First, an exploration of Netflix's 8,807-title catalog. Second, a pricing study that follows monthly plan prices for 10 services from 2011 to 2024.

The question

A subscriber stays on a streaming service when the catalog keeps giving them reasons to stay and the price still feels fair. So I set out to answer two questions with public data:

  • Content: What does Netflix actually offer? How has the catalog grown, which genres dominate, and where do the titles come from?
  • Price: How fast have subscription prices climbed, and is Netflix unusual or part of an industry-wide trend?
弐

The data

DatasetRowsKey columns
netflix_titles.csv8,807type, title, director, cast, country, date_added, release_year, rating, duration, listed_in (genres)
streaming_service.csv777service, date (month), price in USD. 10 services, Jul 2011 to Jan 2024

The catalog covers titles added up to 25 September 2021. The pricing file records one monthly plan price per service per month, so each price hike appears as a step.

参

Cleaning

  • Missing values. director is blank for 2,634 titles (29.9%), and cast and country for about 830 each. I kept these rows and excluded the blanks only from the analysis that needed that column, so I didn't throw away a third of the catalog.
  • Dates. date_added is text like "September 25, 2021". I parsed it with an explicit format; 10 titles had no date.
  • Multi-value cells. listed_in and country hold comma-separated lists, such as "Dramas, International Movies". I split and exploded them so each genre or country is counted once per title.
  • Mixed units. duration means minutes for movies but seasons for TV shows, so I parsed each type separately.
  • A parsing bug I fixed. The original pricing notebook parsed dates with "%b-%y", but the file stores four-digit years ("Jul-2011"). Switching to "%b-%Y" made the whole time series parse correctly.
四

The catalog

Netflix's catalog grew fast and then levelled off.

Insight

From 2019, the mix shifted toward series. Series bring viewers back week after week, while a movie is watched once. That shift fits a retention strategy, though 67% of Netflix's TV shows still have only one season.

A few more facts from the catalog: the median movie runs 98 minutes (half fall between 87 and 114), and the most common rating is TV-MA (3,207 titles), so the catalog leans adult.

五

Pricing

The original notebook compared Netflix, Disney+ and Prime Video, so I started with those three.

Then I extended the study to all 10 services to see whether Netflix was an outlier.

Insight

Netflix isn't an outlier. The cheap-subscription era ended across the industry: 13 of the 16 price hikes in the data happened between January 2022 and January 2024. Newer services launched cheap and then raised prices quickly to catch up.

六

What it means

01 · PRICE

Pay attention to price sensitivity now

With almost every service raising prices at once, switching between them is easy. Price hikes should come with a clear new reason to stay, such as a flagship release or a cheaper ad-supported plan.

02 · CONTENT

Invest in multi-season series

Two-thirds of TV titles end after one season. Shows that keep running give subscribers a reason to stay month after month, which a library of one-off movies doesn't.

03 · MARKETS

Grow with international content

"International" is the largest genre tag, and India is the second-largest producer. Local-language originals are a growth path in markets where price matters most.

04 · MEASURE

Link hikes to cancellations

The next step is to join the price timeline to subscriber cancellation data, so each hike can be judged by how many customers it lost.

七

Limits & next steps

  • No subscriber data. Neither dataset includes viewing or cancellation records, so this study describes what Netflix offers and what it costs. It can't measure who leaves. The telecom case study covers churn modelling directly.
  • One price per service. The pricing file tracks a single plan per service in USD and doesn't name the tier. Ad-supported and regional plans aren't included.
  • Catalog snapshot. The title list stops in September 2021 and shows what was on Netflix at that point, not titles that were later removed.
  • Next: the Random Forest planned in the original project README, plus a dashboard of these charts in Power BI.
